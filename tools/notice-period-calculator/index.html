Resignation and dismissal: two different examples

Six years’ service does not mean six weeks in both directions. The statutory resignation example is one week; the ordinary employer dismissal example is six weeks, subject to contract and exceptions. Probation does not by itself remove statutory notice after one month.

How much notice do I give when resigning after six years?

The general GB statutory minimum is one week after at least one month of employment. Check your resignation clause: it can require longer. Serious breach or an agreed alternative needs separate advice.

How much notice does my employer give after six full years?

For an ordinary GB dismissal or redundancy, the general statutory minimum is six weeks. A longer contractual dismissal clause can apply. Gross misconduct and other exceptions need individual advice.

Does this tool give a definitive last working day?

No. It estimates notice length. Confirm when notice is received, when it starts, whether it is worked or paid in lieu, and any agreed changes. Check wages and holiday separately in your final pay.

Employer dismissal: general statutory rule

  • At least 1 week if employed between 1 month and 2 years.
  • 1 week for each full year if employed between 2 and 12 years.
  • 12 weeks after 12 years of service.
  • Resigning: normally one week after one month’s service; check the resignation clause for any longer period.
